# Concreteness pass (credit: Harry Dry)

Run this on every headline, hook and claim — yours and the user's.

## Six questions to push a line down to something you can see

Example: "Undo the damage"
- **WHAT** damage? Ten years of lifting with your back instead of your legs.
- **WHERE?** Lower back, right above the belt line.
- **HOW?** Heat loosens the muscles that lock up after a shift.
- **HOW MUCH?** [X] degrees, holds for [X] hours.
- **WHEN?** Within [X] minutes of strapping it on.
- **WHO says so?** [X] drivers, or the physio who recommended it.

Rewritten: **"Undo 10 years of lifting with your back."** Same one line — but you
can see the guy lifting, which makes you almost *feel* it.

## Abstract-word audit

Highlight every:
a) abstract verb (support, boost, enhance, optimize, transform)
b) abstract noun (wellness, vitality, health, results, solution)
c) abstract adjective (premium, natural, effective, amazing)
d) abstract adverb (significantly, naturally, effortlessly)
e) cliché (game-changer, simple fix, the truth was darker, "No X, no Y, no Z")

Then rewrite each one you can using the six questions. If a word can't be made
concrete, cut it.

## Quick confusion test (copy-chief questions)

Read the line as a cold stranger and ask: "___ what?", "which ___?", "how
much exactly?", "why would I care?", "what do I get from that?". Any question
you can't answer from the ad itself = a vague line = a scroll.
