# Writing Video Ad Scripts

## Step 1 — Concept (first response)

Unless already clear, ask: "What's the core idea behind this video ad concept?
Please share details about the pain point or desire you want to address, any
particular feature or benefit you want to highlight, the emotional response you
want to evoke, any specific visual elements you're considering, and the specific
action you want viewers to take."

## Step 2 — General or Organic?

**GENERAL — the mini-documentary.** For unaware / problem-aware audiences.
Genuinely interesting content (history, science, a fascinating story) that sells
while it entertains. Brand authority, third-party proof, "lean-in" moments.
Architecture: Hook (fascinating fact/story) → Journey (tension) → Revelation
(universal truth/problem) → Bridge (to their life) → Solution (product as the
modern answer).

**ORGANIC — the camouflaged UGC.** Looks like a real person sharing. Uses the
audience's exact language, natural "imperfections", filler words, spontaneous
reactions ("okay but why though?"), personal discovery ("my friend showed
me…"), platform-native phrasing, regret FOMO ("wish someone told me sooner").
Hidden architecture: relatability → agitate naturally → discovery → believable
transformation → FOMO.

General says "let me tell you an interesting story". Organic says "OMG let me
tell you what just happened to me".

## Step 3 — Voiceover or text-on-screen?

- **Voiceover:** complex stories, mechanisms, emotional depth. ~150–250 words
  per 30–45s.
- **Text-on-screen:** sound-off native feel, visuals carry the story; 5–7 words
  per block; rhythm via CAPS/emphasis. Always include a text hook even on VO ads.

## Step 4 — Deliverable

1. **SIX hooks**, each a different emotional/psychological angle (curiosity loop,
   stakes, emotion, call-out, contrarian, authority/story), all compatible with
   the same HOLD. Each with: spoken line, on-screen text hook, visual
   recommendation (instantly understandable, in safe zones).
2. **ONE HOLD script** (VO or on-screen text only) that works with all six:
   - bridge from hook in natural voice
   - problem/story with stakes (witnesses, social consequence)
   - what made other solutions fail (us vs them)
   - authority BEFORE the product (spoken, specific)
   - unique mechanism in plain spoken language, ingredients tied to it
   - product intro that feels natural (no spec-sheet dialogue)
   - proof: numbers, customer counts, short testimonials
   - future pacing tied to the mechanism, shown not told
   - real urgency (or none) + guarantee in human words
   - CTA framed as the dream state ("Tap below to get your nights back")
3. **Strategic breakdown:** Schwartz principles used, emotional journey map,
   belief ladder (gradualization), identity shift (who they are → become),
   fear/desire balance, curiosity gaps and where each closes, beliefs leveraged.

Then run the Video checklist in `review-playbook.md` on your own draft and fix
it before delivering.

## Core psychology

- **Cold truth:** they've never heard of you, aren't shopping, are avoiding ads,
  will scroll in 1 second. Lead with THEIR world. Two ways in: mirror their
  inner world (problems/desires) or hijack their interests (content they'd watch
  anyway — sled-dog history for dog owners, Olympic secrets for athletes).
- **Emotion first, logic second.** Every line either agitates a pain or paints a
  desire vividly. Sensory language, mental movies.
- **Gradualization / belief ladder.** Start with a truth they accept ("you've
  tried supplements that didn't work") → small logical extension ("what if the
  problem was absorption?") → bigger claim only once prepared. Three channels:
  emotional (understood), logical (makes sense), social (others found it too).
- **Identification.** Every purchase is a vote for an identity. Let them shed
  the painful one: "You're not someone who can't focus — you just haven't had
  the right tools."
- **Information gaps (Zeigarnik).** Open loops, close them, open new ones.
  Specific gaps: "Why your expensive vitamins are creating expensive urine" >
  "the truth about vitamins".
- **Intensification:** claims in action, multiple satisfactions, sensory
  benefits, functional → emotional → social → identity layering.

## Hook principles

The hook is ~80% of the ad. Its only job is to create an emotional state that
makes not watching feel like a mistake.
- Three-element test: **emotional intensity + personal relevance + curiosity gap**.
- Call out exact moments ("that 3pm crash where coffee stops working").
- Challenge current reality ("the real reason you can't focus has nothing to do
  with your phone").
- Vulnerable authenticity ("I spent $3,000 on supplements that did nothing").
- Power words only if the body delivers (exposed, hidden, banned, truth, lie).
- Avoid worn-out hooks for sophisticated markets ("simple fix", "the one thing",
  "the truth was far darker").

## Objections to pre-empt

"I've tried everything" → show how this is fundamentally different (mechanism).
"Too good to be true" → explain why it works + authority. "People will judge
me" → reframe as joining an informed group. "Can't afford to fail again" →
small, risk-free first step. "Not for people like me" → relatable testimonials.

## Research

When language or objections are thin, research: Reddit/forums for exact
phrases, failed solutions, fears; competitor reviews; recent category news.
Use the reader's words. Quality over speed — a mediocre script wastes ad spend.
