# Review Playbook — how the copy chief reviews ads

Extracted from a working copy chief's real feedback on DTC ads (skincare,
probiotics for dogs, women's creatine, side-sleeper pillows, shilajit, fatty
liver strips, hair growth, collagen, weighted blankets for ADHD kids, ingrown
hair roll-ons, native/advertorial ads). Use these as the lens for every review
and as the self-check for everything you write.

---

## The recurring diagnoses (most common first)

1. **Broad + vague.** "These ads try to target too wide an avatar." "This goes
   to every single human who shaves in the world." Fix: pick a SUB-AVATAR, not a
   core avatar, then ONE pain point (e.g. Pilates menopause woman → bloating).
   "Make the ad feel like this product is made just for them." Rule of One.
2. **Weak / missing mechanism.** "'A, B and PHA' isn't going to mean anything to
   most people." "'Billions of probiotics' doesn't build any belief that you're
   different." "If you just make the claim, I can't connect it to your product."
   Fix: a mechanism that's new, concrete, easy for cold audiences, and framed
   us-vs-them: "drugstore washes fail because X → this solves X."
3. **Generic claims anyone can say.** "Gets gunk out of your pores — most
   drugstore washes also say that." "Provides comfort — every single pillow
   says this." "Support for muscles and bones — isn't that what every creatine
   does?" Fix: Falsify test — if a competitor can say it, cut or sharpen it.
4. **No authority, or authority too late / only visual.** "You should signal
   authority before you pitch, not in the CTA." "The authority is only visual —
   there's no moment of her communicating it." Fix: borrowed (study,
   institution, "Korean skincare labs have known this for years"), credential
   ("I've been working with liver herbs for 40 years", a vet), organizational
   (name the research institution behind the stat), social (customer counts).
5. **Empty claims / promises with no proof.** "The timeline section is full of
   promises but zero proof." "10k new hairs and an ingredient I haven't heard of
   doesn't build enough belief." Fix: connect each promise back to the
   mechanism; add "claims clinically backed by [institution]".
6. **Vague proof.** "4 months later their liver numbers had *significantly*
   dropped — what is significantly, how much exactly?" Fix: exact numbers,
   timeframes, sample, source.
7. **Hook problems** (see Video section).
8. **Lecture mode.** "Sounded like a human biology lecture — most people will
   drop off there." "Evolution stuff wouldn't sell me as a parent." Fix: say it
   the way a person would across a table, story-first.
9. **Telling instead of showing.** "'Less snappy in the morning' is TELLING —
   say 'he poured his own cereal without a meltdown'." "Down 40lbs but your hand
   is full of hair?" is showing.
10. **Too much copy / too many ideas.** "Too much text for someone to want to
    read in a static." "Don't dilute the message with 'this helps muscle energy
    and bones too!'" Cut anything that doesn't serve the one angle.
11. **Too product-focused.** "All the product points talk too much about the
    product and not enough about the customer's desires." Translate features
    into felt benefits: not "improves circulation" → "wake up without pain";
    not "removes nerve pressure" → "gives your hand room to rest in its natural
    position".
12. **Fake / unjustified urgency.** "Tap the link before this batch sells out —
    but there isn't any real reason you'd sell out." Fix: give the reason
    (viral in Sweden, limited-time offer with end date, batch size) or drop it.
13. **Compliance.** "Be wary of the word 'fix' — it isn't that compliant."
14. **AI tells.** "'No water, no pill, no mess.' — the 3x 'no' format doesn't
    sound spoken, it sounds really AI." Also cliché ad phrases: "the truth was
    far darker", "simple fix", "here's what helps", "game-changer".

## VIDEO SCRIPT REVIEW checklist

Go section by section. Quote, explain, rewrite.

### Hook (0–3s) — "the hook and hold is the #1 issue" most of the time
- [ ] **Curiosity gap?** Don't give the answer in the hook ("your pores are full
  of oil" → no reason to keep watching). Open a loop the body closes.
- [ ] **Mechanism glimpse / hope** in the hook: "Side sleepers over 45 don't
  realize how fast aligning their neck will [benefit]" > "this simple fix".
- [ ] **Not overused.** Sophisticated audiences have heard "the one thing",
  "simple fix", "the truth was far darker" — find fresher framing.
- [ ] **No fluff.** "3 reasons why I stopped using X" is enough — cut "— and
  what I'm doing instead…". Trim every word that doesn't earn attention.
- [ ] **Emotion + stakes.** "3 reasons why I *hate* [Brand]". "A phase" is too
  soft — needs juxtaposition, specificity, an open loop that demands resolution.
- [ ] **Calls out the sub-avatar.**
- [ ] **Text hook on screen** too (simple AI-avatar "yapper" ads that work
  always have a text hook).
- [ ] **Hook visual is instantly understandable.** A clever x-ray that people
  can't decode = scroll. Test simpler versions.
- [ ] **Awareness match** with body (see `meta-copy-rules.md`).

### Hook → lead bridge
- [ ] Does the lead follow naturally from the hook? Big drop-off between hook
  and lead (e.g. 40% → 11%) = mismatch. Add a bridge in the speaker's natural
  voice ("3 out of 4 probiotics on the shelf" not "76%" from a woman sitting
  in the mud).

### Problem / story
- [ ] Is there a **story** or just education? Pure education is boring unless
  the issue is super relevant.
- [ ] **Witnesses / social stakes.** "Add witnesses into the beach trips — what's
  the worst scenario? A girl rejecting them because of the body they're
  insecure about."
- [ ] **Believable characters.** "A random guy doing all this explanation out of
  nowhere in a store?" — if no one would have this convo, they don't believe.
- [ ] Keep it short — "I got bored, it got too technical".
- [ ] **Reframe the problem** to inspire new hope + validate frustration (e.g.
  "the MRI was done lying down" reassigns why past treatments failed).

### Bridge to product (most common gap in winning-but-not-scaling ads)
- [ ] Explain **what made the other products fail** before showing yours. "You
  told me 74% of probiotics are bad — what made them bad? Educate me so I can
  make sure your product doesn't have it."
- [ ] Don't jump to the product too fast.
- [ ] Root cause: if listing many symptoms (8 dog problems), connect them all
  to ONE root cause the mechanism solves.
- [ ] **Objection handling** for people who already tried the category ("why
  yours works when broad dog probiotics didn't").

### Mechanism
- [ ] New to this market? Easy for cold audiences? Concrete?
- [ ] Ingredients **connected to the mechanism**, not "coming out of nowhere".
- [ ] Ingredient-led benefit claims: "[ingredient] does [felt benefit]".
- [ ] Explained in spoken language, not product spec.

### Authority & proof
- [ ] Authority placed **before** the product pitch.
- [ ] Spoken, not only visual.
- [ ] Specific: institution, year, number, credential.
- [ ] Customer counts / reviews where available.

### Product intro
- [ ] Natural, not "out of nowhere".
- [ ] No spec-sheet dialogue: "A therapeutic aid weighing 2 kilos, distributed
  across the entire little body…" — a mum wouldn't say that face to face.
- [ ] Not hard-sold by a "neutral" narrator ("Ninety nights. You risk nothing."
  sounds affiliated).

### Future pacing / timeline
- [ ] Day-by-day structure is good **if** each step links back to the mechanism.
- [ ] Snappy: "So by 72 hours" → "On day 3". Cut weak beats ("a lot of side
  sleepers say they start dreaming again").
- [ ] Show, don't tell results.

### Close
- [ ] Real reason to act now (see urgency rule) or none.
- [ ] Guarantee mentioned if it exists, in human words: "What got me over the
  line was the 90-day guarantee, so even if it was rubbish I didn't lose
  anything" — not a legal recital.
- [ ] CTA = dream state: "Click below to get your nights back."
- [ ] Spoken: "Sleep with it risk-free for 30 nights and wake up to the morning
  you thought was behind you."

### Language & length
- [ ] Too long for what it communicates? Get to the point faster.
- [ ] Spoken, human, storytelling. Read aloud.
- [ ] Facts not opinions: "Their ingredients just don't cut it anymore" →
  "I noticed they lack some of the most important ingredients, like X and Y."
- [ ] Emotional benefit wording: "get rid of those problems faster" → "stop
  those painful problems faster".
- [ ] No repetition of what the hook already said.
- [ ] Listicles don't need 3 reasons — "if reason 3 is weak, just say 2". Each
  reason must be new (subscribe & save, reviews, better guarantee, more proven
  ingredients, more authority).
- [ ] Faster writing: "One [Competitor] costs 399 SEK — with [Brand] you get TWO
  jars for the same price, and a better result."

### Editing / visuals (flag even in a copy review)
- [ ] Visuals change enough to hold attention (static visuals → low hold).
- [ ] Avatar looks real (fake-looking AI avatar kills trust).
- [ ] Everything inside platform **safe zones**.
- [ ] Native ads need an **x-factor visual** that matches the hook + lead.
- [ ] Simple scripts can beat "over-copywritten" ones in UGC — they feel native.
  Don't over-engineer to the point it stops sounding natural.

### Funnel-stage reality check
- Product-aware ads with empty claims can work as **BOF** (warm audiences
  already believe) — good ROAS but won't scale. Check frequency day by day.
  For TOF/MOF you need mechanism + authority + proof.
- "Turning spend winners into breakthroughs is usually about higher stakes,
  authority, and adding urgency."

## STATIC AD REVIEW checklist

### Readability first — "if a prospect can't understand your ad easily they scroll"
- [ ] **Text size.** "Too much tiny text" — especially for older avatars who'd
  have to pinch their eyes. Often the #1 mistake.
- [ ] **Visual hierarchy** — headline → visual → supporting → CTA, easy to scan.
- [ ] **Amount of text** — cut lines that don't add value ("statements about
  women's life that aren't that relevant").

### Headline
- [ ] **Specific, not vague.** "Show up for yourself" could mean a million
  things. "Run to the bathroom" — first read was coffee. "Smooth what? What
  bumps?" "Who is nobody?" Bumps → "bumps on a car road" without visual context.
- [ ] **Sub-avatar called out** (e.g. side sleepers — "shout about it").
- [ ] **Short.** Cut "your", "started", "tomorrow" if meaning holds.
- [ ] **Show, don't tell** ("Down 40lbs but your hand is full of hair?").
- [ ] **Curiosity over hard sell** ("Here's what you've been missing" > "Here's
  what helps").
- [ ] Consistent with subheadline — no contradiction ("more than creatine" then
  claiming what every creatine does).

### Visual
- [ ] **Shows the problem state and/or dream state** — especially for visual
  problems. "I can't visually see the problem you're solving."
- [ ] **Matches the headline** ("your pillow's flat" + a weird-looking pillow =
  confusion).
- [ ] **Shows how it works** realistically (person sleeping on their side so I
  understand the arm position; not a position real people don't sleep in).
- [ ] Not "product on white background + text" in sophisticated markets — be
  more creative.
- [ ] Product big enough; ingredients in natural form around the bottle.
- [ ] Before/after showing how much the change actually is.
- [ ] No obviously fake AI textures (wrinkly AI skin).

### Benefits & claims
- [ ] Felt benefits, not features or biology words. Ingredient → benefit.
- [ ] Short benefit bullets ("Reduce bloating with [ingredient]").
- [ ] Not claims every competitor makes.
- [ ] Specific numbers ("expensive" → the dollar amount).
- [ ] Explain unknown names ("What is Verisol? Brand or ingredient?").
- [ ] Mechanism visible/understandable, not just asserted.
- [ ] Borrowed authority line for bold claims ("claims clinically backed by
  [institution]").
- [ ] Social proof ("X side sleepers' hand pain relieved").

### Comparison / us-vs-them statics
- [ ] Show more ingredients AND better price per tub together — "more for less".
- [ ] Their side: grey ticks / fewer items, not distracting red dots. Show
  "less ingredients and 2x higher price".

### Funnel fit
- [ ] **Guarantee belongs on the PDP / BOF**, not TOF/MOF problem-solution
  statics — "they're not mentally ready to buy so they wouldn't think of
  guarantees yet; it puts them off".
- [ ] **Add a CTA** ("See how it works").
- [ ] Offer statics: in low-sophistication markets a clear superior offer can be
  enough — keep it simple and readable.

### Testing advice
- [ ] Creatives in a test should be **big visual swings**, not lookalikes.
- [ ] Ad style must fit the product: very visual problems need visual TOF/MOF;
  text-heavy styles may only work BOF.
- [ ] Next round: clearer sub-avatars + angles, no vague copy, a unique
  mechanism, visual problem + dream states.
