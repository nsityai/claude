# Meta Ad Copy Rules

Grounded in Eugene Schwartz's awareness levels, Harry Dry's quality rules and
tested hook frameworks. Make every word earn its place. Every line either builds
curiosity, raises stakes, or moves the reader toward the CTA.

## The Awareness Matching Rule (#1 diagnostic)

Every ad has a hook and a body. They must be at the **same awareness level**.

| Level | What they know | Hook sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Unaware | Don't know they have a problem | "Did you know your dog might be…" |
| 2 Problem aware | Know the problem, not the cause | "If your dog won't stop licking their paws…" |
| 3 Solution aware | Know solutions exist, haven't picked | "Most probiotics only add bacteria…" |
| 4 Product aware | Know your product/category | "Here's why [Brand] works when others don't…" |
| 5 Most aware | Know and love you, need a push | "Still on the fence? Here's the guarantee…" |

Most common failure: a Stage 1 hook on a Stage 4 body — pointing out symptoms to
people who already live with them. Zero curiosity, they scroll.

Fix: match the hook to the body. If the body is product-aware (names brand,
explains mechanism), the hook should be problem- or solution-aware — meet them
where they are and pull them into a reframe they haven't heard.

Diagnosis: (1) What level is the body? (2) What level is the hook? (3) Mismatch
= the problem; rewrite the hook.

Also check **market sophistication** (Schwartz): if the market has heard every
claim, lead with a new mechanism, not a bigger claim.

## The three hook frameworks (best hooks stack 2–3)

### 1. Curiosity loop
Reveal something surprising, withhold the payoff.
- "I almost started TRT injections until I learned this. Here's how I took my
  testosterone from 400 to 800 naturally."
- "Vets won't tell you this, but your dog's itching has nothing to do with
  allergies."
- "There are 3 signs toxins are leaking through your dog's damaged gut wall.
  Number 2 is the one most owners miss."
Rules: the body MUST close the loop. Genuine, not clickbait. One loop per hook.

### 2. Stakes escalation
Make the problem cost more, last longer, spread, have a timeline, or be personal.
- "My dog peed on my floor" → "…on my $10,000 hardwood floors"
- "May lead to dental issues" → "a lifetime of dental issues"
- "Every day you wait, the damage to your dog's gut lining gets worse"
- "I haven't slept through the night in 6 months because of my dog's paw licking"
Rules: escalate the framing, not the facts. The body must pay off the stakes.

### 3. Emotion
Make them feel it before you explain it. Frustration, guilt, exhaustion, love+fear.
- "I've tried everything. Shampoos. Sprays. Three different vets."
- "3am. Again. The sound of licking. Again."
- Visceral: not "my dog itches" → "my dog scratches his ear until it's raw and bleeding"
Rules: follow emotion with real information. One dominant emotion per hook.

## Hook-body payoff rule

If the hook opens a loop, the body must close it. Check: what does the hook
promise? Does the body deliver? If not:
- **Option A:** change the hook to match the body.
- **Option B (usually better):** add 2–3 bridge lines to the body that pay off
  the hook, keep the rest.

## Harry Dry's 3 quality rules

1. **Visualise** — can they SEE it? "Supports digestive health" ✗ → "Seals the
   gut lining so toxins stop leaking through" ✓.
2. **Falsify** — could a competitor say the same thing? "Made with natural
   ingredients" ✗ → "The only chew with a triple-biotic system" ✓.
3. **Nobody else can say** — claims unique to your mechanism, formulation, proof.

## The 12 writing rules

1. One idea per sentence.
2. Specific beats vague ("$400 vet bills", "3am paw licking").
3. No corporate voice. Banned: revolutionary, innovative, premium,
   cutting-edge, game-changer, solution, unlock, elevate, seamless.
4. Short sentences. Fragments are fine.
5. Line one's only job: earn line two.
6. Show, don't claim — show the mechanism that makes it effective.
7. Write how people talk. Read it aloud.
8. Cut every word that doesn't earn its place.
9. Use the reader's language (reviews, Reddit, forums).
10. Agitate before you solve.
11. One CTA. "Tap below." Never two.
12. End on the transformation, not the feature.

## Formats

**Primary text — short (Problem → Mechanism → CTA):** 3–5 lines. L1 hook/problem,
L2–3 mechanism/reframe, L4–5 CTA + offer.

**Primary text — long (Story → Problem → Solution → Proof → CTA):** first-person
testimonial; relatable moment → specific problem detail → product as the
turning point → concrete transformation → offer + CTA.

**"See more" rule:** ~125 characters show before "See more". Front-load the hook.

**Headlines** (below media, above button): under 40 characters; command or
benefit, not description. "Fix the gut. The paw licking stops." (watch
compliance on "fix") / "One daily chew. No more scratching."

## Platform limits (approximate)

| Placement | Limit |
|---|---|
| Meta primary text | ~125 chars before "See more" |
| Meta headline | ~27–40 chars |
| TikTok ad text | ~100 chars; hook in first 3s |
| Google RSA | 30-char headlines, 90-char descriptions |
| Email subject | 30–50 chars |
